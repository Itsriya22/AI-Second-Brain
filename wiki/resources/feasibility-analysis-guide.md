# Feasibility Analysis Guide

A comprehensive guide covering feasibility analysis pillars, business model canvases, market sizing, revenue forecasting, risk management, and AI applications.

Feasibility Analysis
Pillars of Product Success
Desirability
Does the user actually want/need the product?
Will customers pay the proposed price?
Example: Best toaster in the world at $250 vs regular toaster at $8 - most won't pay 250x more
for marginal improvement
Feasibility
Is it technologically possible to build?
Can the organization execute it?
Considers technical, organizational, and resource constraints
Viability
Does it make ﬁnancial sense?
Can both company and customer aﬀord it?

Will it generate sustainable revenue and proﬁt?
Business Model Fundamentals
Deﬁnition
Business model describes how an organization creates, delivers, and captures value
Three Core Components
1. Vision - What is the company's purpose?
2. Delivery - How will the vision be executed?
3. Monetization - How will money be made?
Business Model Canvas vs Lean Canvas
Business Model Canvas youtube link - Business Model Canvas
Explained
Nine building blocks: customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships,
revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partners, cost structure
Better suited for existing companies with established operations


Lean Canvas Model
Modiﬁed version optimized for startups and new products
Provides sequential numbering for systematic completion
Nine sections completed in order:
1. Customer Segments - Who will buy the product?
2. Problem - What issues are customers facing?
3. Existing Alternatives - How do customers solve problems today?
4. Solution - What is your proposed solution?
5. Unique Value Proposition - What makes you diﬀerent?
6. Channels - How will you reach customers?
7. Key Metrics - How will you measure success?
8. Cost Structure - What are your major expenses?
9. Unfair Advantage - What prevents easy copying?
Market Analysis Framework: TAM-SAM-SOM
📊  TAM, SAM, SOM Explained 🚀  | Nail Your Market Sizing Like a Pro!
TAM (Total Addressable Market)
Largest possible market if everyone who could buy actually did
Based on secondary research reports from credible sources (Gartner, Forrester, industry reports)

Example: Global mental health market = $3 billion
SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market)
Portion of TAM you can realistically target
Considers geographic, demographic, and product limitations
Example: India mental health market for working adults = $1.8 billion
SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)
Realistic market share you can capture
Typically 1-10% of SAM
Requires bottom-up validation through multiple calculation methods
Revenue Forecasting Components
Revenue Model Types
Subscription-based - Annual/monthly recurring payments
Usage-based - Pay per use/transaction
Freemium - Basic free, premium paid features
Marketplace - Commission on transactions
Advertising - Revenue from ads
Crowdsourcing/Auctions - Platform facilitation fees
Revenue Calculation Formula
Total Revenue = Unit Price × Number of Units × Conversion Rate
Key Revenue Metrics
ARPU - Average Revenue Per User
CLTV - Customer Lifetime Value
CAC - Customer Acquisition Cost
Churn Rate - Percentage of customers who leave
RPR - Repeat Purchase Rate

Demand Forecasting Methods
Top-Down Approach
Start with total market size
Apply ﬁlters to narrow to addressable market
Estimate market share capture
Bottom-Up Approach
Build from speciﬁc customer segments
Calculate individual segment potential
Aggregate to total market opportunity
Sales Funnel Conversion
Unqualiﬁed Leads →  Qualiﬁed Leads (50% conversion)
Lead to Meetings (30% conversion)
Meetings to Proposal (15% conversion)
Proposal to Win (5% conversion)

Product Development Costs
Market research and idea generation
Design resources and software (CAD/CAM)
Prototyping equipment and materials
R&D and testing expenses
Scaled production costs
Marketing Costs
Market research and campaign development
Media costs (traditional and digital)
SEO, SEM, social media marketing
Sales team salaries and commissions
Channel partner commissions

Other Costs
Capital expenditures (CAPEX)
Operational expenses (OPEX)
Corporate overheads
Intellectual property management
Logistics and warehousing
Cost vs Impact Prioritization Matrix
Four Quadrants
1. High Impact, Low Cost - Prioritize immediately
2. High Impact, High Cost - Plan and justify investment
3. Low Impact, Low Cost - Consider for implementation
4. Low Impact, High Cost - Avoid or delay
Technology Readiness Levels (TRL)
TRL Scale (1-9)
TRL 1-3 - Basic research and proof of concept
TRL 4-6 - Technology development and validation

TRL 7-9 - System demonstration and deployment
Application in Feasibility
Assess technology maturity for deployment decisions
Align expectations across R&D, product, and business teams
Critical for investor communication and roadmapping
Risk and Assumptions Management
Risk Assessment Grid
X-axis: Level of uncertainty
Y-axis: Impact if assumption proves wrong
Prioritize testing high-risk, high-uncertainty assumptions ﬁrst
Validation Methods
Rapid Prototyping - Low-cost mockups and demos
Smoke Testing - Present oﬀer and track engagement
Market Research - Direct customer feedback on pricing and features

Avika Case Study Application
Customer Segments Identiﬁed
1. Patients - Clinically diagnosed mental health issues
2. Non-patients - General population with temporary stress/anxiety
3. Early Adopters - Tech-savvy corporate employees
Problem Areas Addressed
Accessibility to mental health services
Assessment and awareness gaps
Privacy and social stigma concerns
Cost barriers to treatment
Solution Components
Mobile app with curated content
Teleconsulting capabilities
Multi-language support (7 languages)
App-based delivery model

Revenue Model
Annual subscription: ₹1,200 per employee per year
Tiered pricing for company size (10% discounts for larger companies)
Multiple validation methods conﬁrmed 3% market penetration as realistic target
Market Validation Process
1. TAM Calculation - Used Bloom report data: $3 billion India mental health market
2. SAM Reﬁnement - Focused on adults (20-65) and working segments: $1.8 billion
3. SOM Justiﬁcation - Bottom-up analysis targeting speciﬁc industries (IT, BPO, airlines, hotels,
private banks, healthcare)
4. Competition Benchmarking - Compared to international apps (Calm, Lyra) for realistic sizing
Key Assumptions Tested
Corporate employees as primary early adopters
8% market share of psychiatrists as realistic target
3% penetration rate within target corporate segments
Multilingual oﬀering as key diﬀerentiator
